Your Savings Rate Is Your Retirement Date: Find Yours in 2 Minutes

The short version

How much you keep decides when you're free far more than how much you earn. Going from 10% to 15% can bring the date closer by about nine years. And if you're already saving, you're ahead of most people.

Two coworkers. Same age, same job, same paycheck. One of them can stop working decades before the other. They didn't pick different stocks and nobody inherited anything. The difference is one number.

Number 1: your savings rate

Take your take-home pay. Add back anything invested before it reached your account, like a pension contribution or an employer match. Then ask: what share of that went to future you?

Example: you take home 4,000 a month. 400 goes into a retirement plan before payday, so your real pay is 4,400. You also move 200 to an index fund. That's 600 out of 4,400: about 14%.

Two notes people always ask about. Paying down high-interest debt counts as saving, because it's money future you won't have to pay. (That's what most of our first five years looked like.) Rent and car payments don't count. And if your answer today is zero, that's fine. Zero is a row in the table too, and every step from there is real time.

Number 2: your number

The classic rule of thumb is that you can stop depending on a paycheck at around 25 times your yearly spending. Notice that it's based on what you spend, not what you earn. So when you save more, two things happen at once: more money goes in, and the pile you need gets smaller. One move, two wins.

Number 3: the years in between

In 2012 a blogger called Mr. Money Mustache published a table that went viral: starting from zero, the years until your savings can carry you depend mostly on your savings rate.1 Rebuilt with 5% a year after inflation and a target of 25 times spending (Example, a map, not a promise):

  • Save 5% → about 66 years
  • Save 10% → about 52 years
  • Save 15% → about 43 years
  • Save 20% → about 37 years
  • Save 25% → about 32 years
  • Save 30% → about 28 years
  • Save 50% → about 17 years

Look at the jump from 10% to 15%: nine years. From 15% to 20%: another six. The first few points you add are the most valuable points you'll ever save. Long-run real returns of about 5% a year match what world stock markets delivered from 1900 to 2024, but real years jump around, so read the table as a shape, not a schedule.2

Most people won't save 50%, and they shouldn't feel they have to. The point is that every step down the table buys real time, not just real money. In most countries a public pension also arrives on top, which brings the date closer still.3

Already saving? You're ahead

In 2025 the average total savings rate in Vanguard's US workplace plans reached a record 12.1%, counting employer contributions, and 45% of savers raised their rate during the year.4 If you're anywhere near that, you're doing better than you probably feel you are. The next move isn't heroic. It's one or two percent more, automatically.

Your small system: the 2-minute rate check

  1. Open last month's bank statement. Write down take-home pay plus anything saved before payday.
  2. Add up what went to savings, investments and extra debt payments.
  3. Divide. That's your rate. Write it on the first line of your money plan.
  4. Pick next month's rate, one or two points higher, and make it an automatic transfer on payday.
