What high-interest debt actually costs

Here's an Example (not our exact numbers): 30,000 € at 15% a year costs about 4,500 € a year in interest. That's 375 € a month, every month, before a single euro of the loan itself is paid back.

Imagine handing someone 375 € every month and getting nothing back. That's high-interest debt. And you barely feel it, because it happens automatically.

That's the first lesson, and honestly the whole blog in one sentence: whatever runs automatically runs your life. The debt payment was a system someone else designed. The way out was building our own.

It was never about willpower

If you're in debt right now, you might think the problem is you. The research says something kinder.

When money is tight, the brain gets busy. Economists Sendhil Mullainathan and Eldar Shafir call it a "bandwidth tax": scarcity takes up mental space and leaves less for planning ahead.1,2 In one set of experiments, simply thinking about a difficult money problem lowered lower-income participants' scores on reasoning tests by about as much as losing a full night's sleep.2

And if nobody ever taught you this, you're in the majority. In a survey of about 150,000 adults in more than 140 countries, only about one in three could answer basic questions about interest, inflation and spreading risk.3

So we don't sell willpower here. We build small systems: a bill that pays itself, savings that leave your account before you see them, a 48-hour pause before any big purchase. Once they're set up, they work on your worst days too.

What we'll write about

1. Wise choices in everyday life. Groceries, subscriptions, phones, cars, habits. Small decisions repeated a thousand times are where most money quietly goes.

2. The tricks marketing plays on you. Why a price ending in 9 sells more (in one mail-order experiment, the same dress sold better at $39 than at $344), why "sale" doesn't mean cheap, and why so many products are built to be replaced. Once you see a trick, it stops working.

3. Investing and saving. Fees, index funds, emergency funds, the 4% rule, how compounding really works. Every number shown, every source linked.

4. Why all of this matters. Money isn't the goal. Time, calm and choices are. A buffer turns a broken car from a crisis into an annoyance. In the US, only about 63% of adults say they could cover a surprise $400 expense with cash or its equivalent.5 More than a third couldn't.
